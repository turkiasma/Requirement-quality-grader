## Does the judge receive the guide?

Yes. `harness/judge.py` reads `data/labelling_guide.md` at runtime and appends its text to the judge system prompt. The supplied text contains all five definitions, good/bad examples, multi-label guidance, the full 1–5 scoring rubric, and the clarification about main versus secondary issues. Only the paragraph beginning “For the initial dev pilot,” is excluded because it reveals expected item categories. The loaded guide contains 7,840 characters. The original requirement and critique are sent in the user message. Golden label vectors and scorer results are not sent.

The reviewer receives only its shorter v2 prompt, not this guide. Receiving the guide does not establish that the judge follows it. Ollama's inspected requests fitted within its 4,096-token context window; the inspected logs reported no truncation.

## Judge observations — not a reliability measurement

The judge gave 5 to 13/20 critiques (65%) and 4 to 7/20 (35%); none received 1–3. Mean score: 4.65/5. All 15 critiques with label mismatches still received 4 or 5. Nine reasons (45%) repeat the same generic score-5 rubric sentence verbatim without item-specific evidence.

The mean is not evidence of high system quality. Manual inspection for this review found high grades on invented thresholds, incorrect accusations, and rewrites that retain impossible constraints. This review is an assistant analysis, not independent human scoring or a measured human–judge agreement study. The same underlying model serves reviewer and judge; shared errors are a possibility to investigate, not a bias result established by this run.

## Recommended next refinement, without changing this baseline

1. **Validate the policy and reference labels.** Have humans review disputed labels and clarify placeholder grading. A placeholder is allowed to avoid inventing information, but the rubric's “fully fixes” language leaves uncertainty about the highest eligible grade when a stakeholder decision remains open. Do not automatically declare every score of 4 on a placeholder wrong.
2. **Reviewer v3 experiment, with judge v2 held fixed.** Supply the actual criterion decision boundaries from the guide, excluding pilot labels and critique-scoring instructions. Emphasize one obligation plus its conditions/fields is atomic; missing implementation detail is not automatically incompleteness; impossible claims can still be objectively disproved; explicitly missing parameters differ from ambiguous wording. Require each reported issue to justify a real violation, not a preference. Preserve supplied values and use named placeholders or clarification requests rather than inventing thresholds, formats, actors, or policies. Inspect whether the rewrite removes each detected problem; mark impossible goals as needing a changed constraint instead of pretending to satisfy them.
3. **Judge v3 experiment on frozen critiques.** Reuse these exact saved reviewer outputs rather than regenerating them while testing the judge. Instruct the judge to assess the original requirement independently, verify each accusation, compare new rewrite facts with the input, and check the rewrite for remaining defects. Require a concrete reason tied to the actual critique, not a restatement of the grade definition. Apply the existing score meanings and main/secondary distinction; do not award 4/5 to unsupported accusations or unresolved major defects simply because the rewrite sounds polished.
4. **Compare with human grades.** Start with a small varied selection covering good requirements, invented values, missed main issues, valid placeholders, and infeasible rewrites. Humans grade without seeing judge scores. Record before/after counts and percentages, and human disagreement before reconciliation. No automatic score cap or new rubric is silently imposed in this review.

Dataset expansion and formal bias experiments can follow this calibration work. Do not treat these dev results as held-out test performance, and do not interpret prompt changes as improvements until they have been measured.
